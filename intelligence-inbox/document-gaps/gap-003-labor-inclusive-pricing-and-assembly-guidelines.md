# Gap 003 – Labor-Inclusive Pricing and Assembly Guidelines

**Date Logged:** 30-06-2026
**Logged By:** Vishnusri
**Status:** Open
**Classification:** Document Gap

---

## Gap Summary

No documented business process currently exists within the Postage AIOS or wider LEDSone operational documentation to include warehouse picking time and manual assembly effort when determining product selling prices. The gap between fulfilment labour cost and selling price for assembly-intensive products has been identified through a recurring operational issue (Issue 004), but no guidance document, pricing framework, or assessment process has been found that addresses this.

---

## Current Situation

Warehouse staff manually assemble selected products that require multiple individual components to be picked separately and assembled by hand before packing and dispatch. This process is occurring as part of daily fulfilment operations.

Pricing decisions for these products appear to be made independently of warehouse operations, without a structured mechanism for fulfilment effort to be communicated to or considered by the teams responsible for setting selling prices. As a result, products that require significantly more labour to fulfil are priced the same way as simpler products, with no adjustment for the additional warehouse cost involved.

The absence of documented guidance means there is no agreed method for identifying which products are labour-intensive, no standard way of measuring that labour, and no process for feeding fulfilment cost data into pricing decisions.

---

## Missing Documentation

The AIOS currently lacks documented guidance for the following:

| Missing Document | Description |
|-----------------|-------------|
| **Labour-inclusive pricing guidance** | No documentation exists defining how warehouse picking time and assembly effort should be factored into product selling prices |
| **Assembly effort assessment** | No documented method exists for assessing or recording the effort required to assemble a product before dispatch |
| **Picking complexity assessment** | No documented framework exists for classifying products by picking complexity (e.g., single-item vs multi-component) |
| **Labour cost estimation** | No documented process exists for estimating or recording the warehouse labour cost per unit for fulfilment of a specific SKU |
| **High labour SKU identification** | No documented process exists for identifying, flagging, and monitoring SKUs that require above-average fulfilment effort |
| **Pricing review workflow** | No documented workflow exists for escalating fulfilment cost concerns to the teams responsible for pricing decisions |

---

## Business Impact

The absence of this documentation results in the following consequences:

| Impact | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Pricing decisions without warehouse input** | Product prices are set without access to structured data on fulfilment labour, meaning assembly-intensive products are routinely underpriced |
| **High labour products remaining underpriced** | Without a review mechanism, products that cost more to fulfil than their price recovers continue to be sold at a loss on the labour component indefinitely |
| **Reduced operational efficiency** | Without a classification or flagging system, warehouse operations cannot prioritise process improvements for the highest-impact SKUs |
| **Financial leakage** | Every unit of a labour-intensive product dispatched at an unrecovered labour cost represents a direct financial loss that accumulates at scale |

---

## Required Documentation

The following documentation is recommended for creation once the root cause investigation under Issue 004 has produced sufficient data:

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Labour-inclusive pricing principles** | Define the principle that warehouse fulfilment labour should be included as an input to product pricing decisions |
| **Warehouse fulfilment costing guidance** | Provide a method for calculating the warehouse cost per unit based on picking time, assembly time, packing time, and labour rate |
| **Assembly complexity matrix** | Define categories of assembly complexity (e.g., no assembly, light assembly, full manual assembly) with criteria for each |
| **Picking complexity classification** | Define categories of picking complexity (e.g., single item, 2–3 components, 4+ components) with criteria and example SKUs |
| **High labour SKU review process** | Define the process for identifying, flagging, escalating, and reviewing SKUs that exceed a defined labour threshold |
| **Pricing review checklist** | Define the checklist for a structured pricing review that includes fulfilment cost as a mandatory input |

> **Note:** These documents should not be created until the time study and cost review recommended in Issue 004 have been completed. Documentation created before accurate data is available may embed incorrect cost assumptions.

---

## Recommended AIOS Assets

Once investigation and data collection under Issue 004 are complete, the following AIOS assets are recommended for future creation:

| Asset | Type | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| **Labour-Intensive SKU Registry** | Reference document | A maintained list of SKUs identified as requiring above-average fulfilment labour, with component counts and estimated assembly time |
| **Warehouse Fulfilment Cost Matrix** | Reference document | A matrix mapping product categories to their estimated fulfilment cost per unit, updated periodically as labour rates and product mixes change |
| **Assembly Complexity Guide** | Skill or context document | Step-by-step guidance for warehouse staff on how to assess and record the assembly complexity of a product at the point of fulfilment |
| **Pricing Review Workflow** | Skill document | A defined workflow for submitting fulfilment cost data to the pricing team and tracking the outcome of pricing review requests |
