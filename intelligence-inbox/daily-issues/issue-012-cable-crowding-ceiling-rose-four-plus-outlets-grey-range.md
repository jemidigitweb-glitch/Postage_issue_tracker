# Issue 012 – Cable Crowding in Ceiling Roses with More Than Four Outlets

**Date Logged:** 2026-07-07
**Logged By:** Vishnusri
**Status:** Open — Under Investigation
**Classification:** Daily Issue

---

## Issue Summary

Ceiling roses with more than four outlets do not provide sufficient internal space to
accommodate the standard cable configuration during assembly. When standard components are
used, cables curl inside the rose due to the restricted internal clearance. This affects
assembly quality and consistency for multi-colour spider pendant light orders.

The warehouse team has identified that grey range components provide the additional clearance
needed to prevent cable crowding in these configurations and is currently using them as an
operational practice. However, this requirement has not been standardised in the product
listing or the picking and fulfilment workflow, meaning it is not consistently applied across
all staff and all orders for ceiling roses with more than four outlets.

---

## Business Problem

The core problem is a mismatch between the internal space available in ceiling roses with more
than four outlets and the cable configuration used during standard assembly:

- **Ceiling roses with more than four outlets have insufficient internal space.** When the
  standard cable configuration is applied, the cables crowd inside the rose and curl during
  assembly. This is a confirmed physical constraint of these ceiling rose variants.
- **Grey range components are required but not standardised.** The warehouse team has
  identified that grey range components resolve the clearance problem. However, this
  requirement has not been formally incorporated into the product listing or the picking
  and fulfilment process.
- **The grey range solution is applied inconsistently.** Because the requirement exists as
  operational knowledge rather than a documented process, it depends on individual staff
  awareness. Different staff members may handle the same ceiling rose configuration
  differently, producing inconsistent assembly results.
- **The product listing does not reflect the correct component requirement.** Any staff member
  following the standard listing or picking instruction for a ceiling rose with more than four
  outlets will select the standard configuration rather than the grey range components, and will
  encounter the cable crowding problem.
- **The fulfilment workflow has not been updated.** No step in the current picking and
  fulfilment process prompts staff to select grey range components when ceiling roses with more
  than four outlets are present in an order.

---

## Current Operational Process

The current warehouse fulfilment process for multi-colour spider pendant light orders is as
follows:

1. **Order received for multi-colour spider pendant light** — an order arrives requiring
   assembly of a spider pendant with the specified ceiling rose and associated coloured cables.
2. **Ceiling rose and coloured cables are selected** — warehouse staff select the required
   ceiling rose and the coloured cables for assembly based on the order specification and the
   current product listing.
3. **Assembly is attempted using the standard configuration** — when a ceiling rose with more
   than four outlets is selected, standard components are used by default unless the staff
   member is individually aware of the grey range requirement.
4. **Cable crowding occurs** — the cables curl inside the ceiling rose due to insufficient
   internal space. This produces an assembly result that does not meet the required quality
   standard and requires rework or disassembly.
5. **Grey range components are substituted** — staff who are aware of the operational practice
   substitute grey range components to resolve the clearance problem and complete the assembly.

**Failure point:** Steps 3 and 4 represent the failure in the current process. The grey range
requirement exists as informal operational knowledge but is not embedded in the product listing,
picking instruction, or fulfilment workflow. Assembly quality for ceiling roses with more than
four outlets therefore depends on individual staff awareness rather than a documented and
enforced process step.

## Root Cause

**Not Yet Confirmed.**

The available evidence confirms that ceiling roses with more than four outlets have
insufficient internal space for the standard cable configuration, and that grey range
components resolve the crowding problem. However, the reason why the grey range requirement
has not been standardised in the product listing and fulfilment workflow has not been
confirmed and requires investigation.

The following possibilities exist but none have been confirmed:

- The grey range requirement may have been identified informally and communicated verbally
  without being recorded in the product listing or workflow documentation
- The product listing may have been created before the physical space constraint was
  identified, and has not since been reviewed for this configuration
- No formal validation process may exist that checks assembly clearance before a product
  configuration is approved for the picking and fulfilment workflow

No assumption in this list should be treated as fact. Investigation is required before any
corrective action is finalised.

## Existing Workaround

The warehouse team is currently using grey range components for ceiling roses with more
than four outlets as an operational practice. Staff who are aware of the physical space
constraint substitute grey range components during assembly to provide adequate internal
clearance and prevent cable crowding.

This is the current operational practice in use by team members who have identified the
issue. It is not a formally documented or standardised workaround — the product listing
continues to specify the standard configuration, and the picking and fulfilment workflow
does not include a step directing staff to select grey range components for these ceiling
rose variants.

The effectiveness of this practice depends on individual staff awareness and cannot be
relied upon to produce consistent assembly results until the requirement is formally
standardised and communicated to all warehouse staff.

---

## Recommended Next Actions

1. **Standardise the grey range requirement** — formally specify that grey range components
   must be used for all ceiling roses with more than four outlets. Record this as a confirmed
   assembly requirement rather than an informal practice.
2. **Update the product listing** — amend the product listing for ceiling roses with more
   than four outlets to specify grey range components as the required assembly component
   for these configurations.
3. **Update the picking and fulfilment process** — add a step or prompt to the picking and
   fulfilment workflow that directs warehouse staff to select grey range components whenever
   a ceiling rose with more than four outlets is present in an order.
4. **Communicate the requirement to all warehouse staff** — brief all staff who may assemble
   multi-colour spider pendant orders on the grey range requirement, so that the correct
   component is consistently selected regardless of individual prior knowledge.
5. **Validate all ceiling rose configurations with five or more outlets** — conduct a
   physical validation of each affected ceiling rose variant to confirm which configurations
   are affected, what the correct grey range specification is for each, and whether any
   additional configurations require the same or a different resolution.
6. **Confirm the root cause** — investigate why the grey range requirement was not
   standardised when the physical space constraint was identified, to determine whether a
   process or documentation control is needed to prevent similar assembly configuration
   errors in future.
