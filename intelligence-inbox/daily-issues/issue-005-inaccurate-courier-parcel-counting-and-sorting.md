# Issue 005 – Inaccurate Courier Parcel Counting and Sorting

**Date Logged:** 30-06-2026
**Logged By:** Vishnusri
**Status:** Open — Under Monitoring
**Classification:** Daily Issue

---

## Issue Summary

Parcels intended for different couriers — DPD, EVRi (Hermes), and Royal Mail — are not being consistently counted or separated correctly during morning dispatch preparation. Staff are expected to count and segregate parcels by courier before dispatch, but the counts produced are sometimes inaccurate and parcels are occasionally found mixed between courier groups.

Incorrect parcel counts create confusion at the point of dispatch and require additional manual searching to locate and correctly assign parcels to the right courier batch. This adds time to the morning dispatch process and introduces the risk that parcels may be presented to or collected by the wrong courier.

---

## Business Problem

Incorrect parcel counts and mixed courier batches reduce dispatch efficiency and increase the workload placed on warehouse supervisors and staff. When counts do not match the expected number of parcels per courier, the team must stop the dispatch process, manually recount, and search the warehouse to identify any misplaced items.

Beyond the operational disruption, mixed courier batches carry the risk of a parcel being dispatched through the wrong courier entirely — which could result in delivery failures, customer complaints, and additional cost to retrieve and rebook the shipment. The issue is recurring and has persisted despite repeated verbal reminders to staff, indicating that verbal instruction alone is insufficient to ensure consistent execution.

---

## Current Operational Process

The morning dispatch preparation process currently operates as follows:

1. **Morning labelling** — labels are printed and applied to outbound parcels
2. **Parcel counting** — staff count the total number of parcels ready for dispatch
3. **Courier identification** — parcels are expected to be identified and grouped by their assigned courier (DPD, EVRi/Hermes, or Royal Mail)
4. **Dispatch preparation** — grouped parcels are staged in the relevant area ready for courier collection
5. **Courier collection** — each courier collects their respective batch of parcels

Staff are expected to count parcels separately for each courier as part of the dispatch preparation step, producing an accurate count per courier before collection begins. In practice, this step is not being executed consistently, resulting in inaccurate counts and occasionally mixed batches.

## Existing Workaround

Supervisors manually recount parcels and search the warehouse whenever a reported count does not match the expected number for a given courier. This ensures the correct count is confirmed and misplaced parcels are located before courier collection takes place.

The workaround is effective in preventing incorrect dispatch on a case-by-case basis, but it does not address the root cause. It places additional responsibility on supervisors, consumes time during a period of the day when operational pace is highest, and does nothing to prevent the same counting errors from recurring the following morning.

---

## Recommended Next Actions

The following actions are recommended to monitor and address the issue:

1. **Observe the morning counting process** — conduct direct observation of the morning parcel counting and sorting process to understand where inconsistencies are occurring and identify any specific steps or individuals where the breakdown happens
2. **Reinforce courier segregation** — reinforce the expectation that parcels must be physically separated by courier group before counting begins, so that counting and segregation are treated as a single step rather than two separate activities
3. **Review staffing requirements** — assess whether the volume of morning dispatch preparation work is appropriate for the number of staff allocated to the task, as time pressure may be contributing to counting errors
4. **Monitor counting accuracy** — record daily parcel counts per courier alongside the actual number collected, and track discrepancies over a defined monitoring period to quantify the frequency and scale of the issue
5. **Validate dispatch counts before courier collection** — implement a simple count confirmation step — such as a supervisor sign-off on the count per courier — before any courier is permitted to begin collection
