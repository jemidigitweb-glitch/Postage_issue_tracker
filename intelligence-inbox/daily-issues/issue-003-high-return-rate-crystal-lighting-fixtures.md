# Issue 003 – High Return Rate for Crystal Lighting Fixtures

**Date Logged:** 30-06-2026
**Logged By:** Vishnusri
**Status:** Open — Under Investigation
**Classification:** Daily Issue

---

## Issue Summary

Crystal Lighting Fixture models Crystal 120 and Crystal 160 are experiencing recurring weekly returns of approximately 10–15 units. Returns are being received and processed by the warehouse on a repeated basis.

Although inventory adjustments are performed each time a return is received, the underlying operational problem has not been identified or resolved. The same products continue to be returned week after week, indicating a systematic issue rather than isolated incidents.

---

## Business Problem

The warehouse is repeatedly processing the same returned products without any structured investigation into the permanent cause. Each time a Crystal 120 or Crystal 160 unit is returned, it is received, manually adjusted in inventory, and stored — after which the cycle repeats the following week.

This creates unnecessary operational effort and results in the same inventory being handled multiple times across multiple cycles. Without identifying the root cause, the team continues to absorb the cost and time of the same recurring process indefinitely.

---

## Current Operational Process

Returned Crystal 120 and Crystal 160 units currently follow this process:

1. **Received** — returned units arrive at the warehouse and are accepted
2. **Manually adjusted** — inventory records are updated manually to reflect the returned stock
3. **Stored** — units are placed back into warehouse storage
4. **Processed for future handling** — units are held for potential resale, write-off, or re-dispatch as determined at the time

No structured root cause investigation currently exists. Returns are processed as individual transactions without any mechanism to identify patterns, track return reasons, or escalate recurring issues for resolution.

## Existing Workaround

The current workaround is manual inventory and account adjustment after each product return is received. Each returned Crystal 120 or Crystal 160 unit is processed individually, and the inventory record is corrected manually.

This workaround balances the inventory record at the time of processing but does not resolve the recurring issue. The same cycle repeats the following week. The workaround manages the symptom without addressing the cause.

---

## Recommended Next Actions

The following actions are recommended before any documentation or procedure decisions are made:

1. **Investigate root cause** — initiate a structured investigation to determine why Crystal 120 and Crystal 160 units are being returned at a higher than expected rate
2. **Perform quality inspection** — physically inspect a sample of returned units to identify any visible quality, assembly, or packaging defects
3. **Track return reasons** — begin recording the stated reason for each return at the point of receipt to build evidence for pattern analysis
4. **Review packaging** — assess whether current packaging for these models is adequate to prevent transit damage
5. **Monitor affected SKUs** — flag Crystal 120 and Crystal 160 in the warehouse system for closer monitoring over the next four weeks to capture accurate volume data

> **Note:** No new procedures, SOPs, or AIOS documents should be created at this stage. The root cause has not been identified. Document or procedure recommendations are premature until the investigation produces findings.
