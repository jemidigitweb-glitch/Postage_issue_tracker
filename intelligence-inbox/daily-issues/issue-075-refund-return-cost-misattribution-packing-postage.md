## Issue Summary

By the Sales team's day-to-day estimate, around 70% of refund and return cases trace back to packing and postage-related issues (damage in transit, incorrect packaging, mislabelling, carrier handling) rather than pricing, listing accuracy, or customer intent. If this expense is folded into "net sales" calculations and attributed against Sales performance without separating out its actual root cause, it distorts the picture of what Sales pricing decisions are actually achieving.

---

## Current Operational Process

Refund and return costs are understood to be included as an expense component within "net sales" calculations, without root-cause separation by category (packing/postage-driven vs. pricing- or demand-driven).

---

## Business Problem

Refund/return costs caused by packing and postage issues are, by the Sales team's estimate, a substantial share (~70%) of all refund/return cases, yet are not distinguished from pricing- or demand-driven returns when net sales are calculated and used to judge Sales pricing decisions.

---

## Root Cause

**Status: Not Yet Confirmed**

### Confirmed Facts

Refund/return expense is a cost component that can be folded into "net sales" calculations (see issue-070).

### Assumptions

The ~70% estimate for packing/postage-driven refund and return cases (damage in transit, incorrect packaging, mislabelling, carrier handling) is described explicitly as the Sales team's day-to-day estimate and has not yet been formally validated against actual return-reason data.

---

## Business Impact

If unvalidated, this misattribution risks Sales pricing decisions being judged against an expense figure that is largely driven by packing/postage execution rather than by the price or listing itself.

---

## Operational Risks

Continued blending of packing/postage-driven and pricing/demand-driven return costs will keep distorting net sales figures used to evaluate Sales performance, and may also obscure the true scale of packing/postage quality issues from the teams responsible for fixing them.

---

Not Available in the Evidence.

## Recommended Next Actions

Validate the ~70% estimate against actual return-reason data, and establish root-cause attribution on refund/return costs — separating packing/postage-driven returns from pricing- or demand-driven returns — so expense is assigned to the function actually responsible, before it is used to judge pricing outcomes.
