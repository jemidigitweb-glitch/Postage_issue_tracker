## Issue Summary

Beyond pricing itself, the absence of a real-time way to validate profitability against Finance's cost model is stopping the Sales team from introducing any new pricing tactic, listing approach, or promotional method, because there is no way to know in advance whether it will generate a positive or negative result.

---

## Current Operational Process

New Sales initiatives (pricing tactics, listing approaches, promotional methods) can only be judged "positive" or "negative" once Finance's profitability review is delivered — up to 6 months after the initiative was launched.

---

## Business Problem

By the time an initiative's profitability is confirmed, it has either already caused damage or the window to build on it has passed. This is not attributed to a lack of ideas or effort — it is the absence of a real-time validation mechanism that is preventing the Sales team from innovating at all.

---

## Root Cause

**Status: Not Yet Confirmed**

### Confirmed Facts

There is no fast or lightweight way to pre-validate a new sales initiative against Finance's cost model before rollout. The same underlying gap described in issue-070 (no documented net sales formula) and issue-071 (delayed feedback loop) applies here: without a formula or a fast feedback mechanism, no initiative can be tested with confidence.

Not Available in the Evidence.

## Business Impact

The Sales team's ability to grow sales through new approaches is limited, independent of the direct pricing/ranking impact described elsewhere — this is a separate, ongoing opportunity cost.

---

## Operational Risks

Continued avoidance of new pricing tactics, listing approaches, or promotional methods by the Sales team, since any one of them could take up to 6 months to be confirmed as positive or negative.

---

## Existing Workaround

Not Available in the Evidence.

---

## Recommended Next Actions

Establish a fast, lightweight way to pre-validate new sales initiatives against the cost model before rollout — even a simple estimated impact check — so the team can test and commit to new approaches without waiting 6 months to learn whether the decision was right.
